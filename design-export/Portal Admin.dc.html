PORTAL · THE 4 SEPTEMBER SPEC

Point rules, club facts, scoring, roles, content and members

The surfaces the approved spec adds on top of the fifteen screens in Portal. Same dark chrome, same tables, same tokens — these are new rooms in the same building, not a second product.

The organising idea across all of them: points are either arithmetic or a judgement, and the two never share a control. Eleven of the thirteen categories in the points document compute from data the district already holds, so they are displayed with a trace and no input box. The twelfth and thirteenth are a single field with a written reason attached to a name.

Period is the dimension that is easy to miss. The points document mixes monthly, yearly and one-time rules without saying so — attendance resets each month, retention accumulates across the year, and a vocational centre pays once however many times the fact is touched. Every rule row states its period, because a rule with the wrong one silently produces wrong totals for eleven months.

Two guardrails. A rule edit applies to future months only, so no club's settled score moves under it; and every change is written to the audit log with the editor's name, because points decide district awards.

Scope is the whole mechanism. A ZRR is not a different kind of officer with a filtered list — it is the officer role scoped to one zone, so every screen already built shows less without being rewritten. The same applies to a subdomain lead club: scoped to one project, nothing else changes.

One gap the spec names and defers. Roles have no effective dates, so when a president changes mid-year — which happened this year — last month's report attributes to whoever holds the role today. Until that is fixed, the audit log is the only record of who actually filed what.

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

1

You send it

Now

2

Your president is told

Not asked to approve — just told, so nothing is submitted behind their back

3

An officer edits and publishes

Within about a week. They may tighten the wording; they will not change what you claim

4

It appears on the showcase

With your club named, and your name if you want it

Why a member submits, not the president

The avenue directors run the projects. Routing every showcase entry through the president makes one person the bottleneck for thirty-eight people's work, and it means the write-up comes from somebody who was not there.

Publishing still needs an officer, so the gate the district asked for is intact. What changes is who fills the form — and the description is better for it.

The consent tick is not boilerplate

It is the one thing a member can confirm and a district officer cannot. An officer approving a photograph of a school handover has no way to know whether those parents agreed; the person who was in the room does.
