On email OTP as the primary second factor. It removes the thing that actually broke the authenticator route: there is no enrolment step. A new president signs in on day one with an address the district already holds, and nobody has to be walked through installing an app or re-enrolling a lost phone. The repo currently ships an RFC 6238 authenticator engine in src/lib/totp.js, so moving to email means that path becomes dead code or an opt-in second option — worth deciding rather than leaving both half-wired. Two design consequences: the code needs a generous expiry (ten minutes, not thirty seconds) because email is slow and people switch apps to fetch it, and it needs a resend that is visibly rate-limited, or a president who doesn't see the mail will click it eleven times and invalidate their own code. Everything else holds — autofocus, paste all six at once, and the five-hour device memory already in App.jsx, so a president signs in about once a month.

A club that did nothing in a month still files the month — "no activities in August" is a valid one-click submission and keeps the compliance record honest. A missing report and an empty one mean very different things to the secretariat.

Twelve fields down to five

The reporting workspace in PortalPage.jsx asks twelve numbered questions per project — event name, date, venue, area of focus, club strength, initiated by, collaborating organisations, officials present, beneficiary count, a forty-word description, a Rotary Service Project Center URL and a Google Drive link — six of them required. Club-submitted forms were called error-prone in the planning call, and twelve fields per project is where that comes from. Five survive per activity: title, date, area of focus, reach, and collaborators. Venue and club strength move to optional, asked only if the officer queries the report. The Service Project Center URL and Drive link move to the review step, where they belong — they are filing metadata, not something a president has to hand before describing what they did. And the forty-word description is dropped entirely from submission: the officer rewrites club-submitted summaries anyway before publishing, which is exactly what the showcase queue screen does. Every field removed is a report that actually arrives — and since a month often holds four or five activities, a field cut here is cut four or five times over.
