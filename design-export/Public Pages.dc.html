DISEASE PREVENTION AND TREATMENT

Mahadan blood donation camp

Nine clubs ran a single-day camp at four venues across South Delhi with Rotary Blood Bank. Donors were pre-registered through club WhatsApp groups to keep queues short.

The camp ran from eight in the morning until six, with registration desks at each venue staffed by two Rotaractors on rotation. Rotary Blood Bank provided the collection teams and transport; clubs handled outreach, refreshments and the donor queue.

Pre-registration through WhatsApp turned out to be the difference. In previous years donors arrived unannounced and waited upwards of an hour; this time the longest recorded wait was eleven minutes. Clubs planning a similar camp should budget one registration volunteer per forty pre-registered donors.

Follow-up: donors who deferred on medical grounds were added to a list for the next camp rather than being turned away without a next step.

Privacy policy

Rotaract District 3011 holds personal information about roughly three hundred members across 75 clubs. This page says what we hold, why, who can see it, and how to have it removed. It is written to be read rather than to be defensible.

What we hold

Your name, club, email address and phone number, because a district cannot run without being able to reach its office bearers.

Your photograph, blood group, skills and interests — only if you or your club entered them. None of these are required to hold an account.

A record of the district events you checked in to, and any contribution hours an officer has approved.

For patients recorded under Project Drishti: name, age, contact and clinical need. This is held by the lead club and the district only, is never published in any form, and is entered only where consent was taken and recorded.

Who can see it

Your own club’s president and secretary see your full record.

District officers see records across all clubs, because scoring and compliance require it.

Other members see only your name, club, zone, photograph and skills through the district directory — never your contact details, and never anything about points.

The public site shows office bearers’ names and, where a club has provided them, a contact route. It shows no member’s personal details.

What we do not do

We do not sell or share your information with anyone outside Rotary International structures.

We do not publish blood groups publicly. They exist so that a district of young people can find a donor quickly, and they stay behind a login.

We do not use your data to train anything. The one place this product uses a language model is an officer reading club-submitted report notes; no personal record is sent to it.

Photographs and files

Images on this site are links to Google Drive or Google Photos albums that the district or a club already owns. We cache them; we do not hold a second copy. If you want a photograph of yourself removed, it has to be removed from the source album, and we will unlink it here the same day.

Removing your information

Ask your club president or write to the district secretariat. An account is closed and its personal fields cleared within seven days.

Two things survive: the fact that a report was filed, and the fact that a club’s member attended an event. Both are aggregate compliance records the district is required to keep, and neither carries your name after closure.

Questions, or something you want removed

Rtr. Sarthak Bansal, District Secretary — the same person who handles club records. Replies usually within three days.

Write to the secretariat

Terms of service

This site is run by Rotaract District Organization 3011, a district of Rotary International covering Delhi and the National Capital Region. Using it — browsing, or holding a portal account — means accepting what follows. It is short on purpose.

Who can hold an account

Portal accounts belong to members of clubs chartered in this district, and are created either by a club officer or by signing up and being approved by your own club.

An account is personal. Sharing a login with the next year's office bearers instead of handing over properly is the single most common way district records get attributed to the wrong person.

Accounts follow the role, not the person. When your term ends, access to club and district data ends with it.

What you put here

You keep whatever you write and whatever photographs you submit. By submitting them you give the district permission to publish them on this site and its channels, and to keep them as part of the district record.

Only submit photographs you have the right to submit, and where the people in them are content to appear on a public page. Projects involving children or patients need that permission taken in person, not assumed.

The district may edit a summary for length and clarity before publishing. It will not change what a project claims to have done.

What we ask of you

Report honestly. Reach figures, unit counts and attendance drive district awards, and a number invented to look better takes an award from a club that earned it.

Do not use the member directory to sell anything, recruit for anything outside Rotaract, or contact a member who has asked you not to.

Do not attempt to reach data outside your own club and role. Every mutation is checked server-side; attempts are logged.

What we do not promise

This site is run by volunteers on free infrastructure. It will occasionally be slow or briefly unavailable, and images hosted on external albums may break when somebody reorganises a Drive folder.

Career Bridge listings are posted by Rotarians and verified by a phone call. That call is not an endorsement of an employer, and the district is not party to anything that follows between you and a company.

Points are assigned by district officers using published methodology. They are a judgement, they are auditable, and they are final once a Rotary year closes.

Ending it

You can close your account at any time by asking your club president or the secretariat — see the privacy policy for what happens to your data.

The district may suspend an account for misuse of the directory, for repeated false reporting, or at a club's request when someone ceases to be a member.

Governing law and disputes

Indian law, courts at New Delhi. Anything about points, awards or club standing is settled inside Rotaract first — through your club, then your zone representative, then the District Rotaract Representative. That route exists and works, and it is faster than any other.
