RAC DISTRICT 3011 · DESIGN RECONCILIATION 4 September 2026

WHAT IS DESIGNED, WHAT IS NOT, WHAT NEEDS A DECISION

The design against the 4 September spec

Two canvases exist. Public Pages holds 21 public screens, Portal holds 15, each drawn at 1440, 768 and 390. Both were designed against features.md and the planning call, and against that brief they are close to complete.

The admin-portal spec approved on 4 September is a much larger brief. It opens the portal to every member across 75 clubs, replaces four fixed roles with configurable scoped ones, makes both the report form and the point rules editable without a deploy, adds event attendance capture, and adds a fifth subdomain. Roughly two thirds of it has no design yet.

This document maps every part of both specs to a screen, states plainly what is missing, and separates decisions I can make from decisions the district has to make.

3 · What the point matrix does to reporting

You asked for reporting to be designed so the district team can award points easily. Reading the spec, the honest answer is that most points should never be awarded by a person at all — the official document's 13 categories are mostly arithmetic, and the spec already says rules compute the moment a report is submitted. Design's job is therefore not a better scoring form. It is to make the two kinds of points visibly different.

COMPUTED — SHOWN, NOT EDITED

Workshop counts, attendance brackets, retention percentages, collaboration tiers, dues timing, RIDE hosting days. These arrive already totalled. An officer's screen should display them with the rule that produced each one, and offer no input box — an editable field next to a computed number invites an officer to "correct" arithmetic, and the audit log then records a human overriding a rule for no stated reason.

JUDGED — THE ONLY PLACE A PERSON TYPES

Quality of a collaboration, the effort log, anything the document has no category for. One input, one note field, one name attached. Small by design: the call was explicit that automated quality scoring is not wanted, and equally explicit that scoring must not become a full-time job.

Three consequences for screens already drawn:

  • The scoring table changes shape. Today it has one input per club-month. It needs a computed subtotal per category, a single judged input, and a rule trace an officer can open when a club queries a number.
  • Club facts get their own screen. Dues date, RI Citation, Paul Harris Fellows, dual membership, MDIO committee, sister-club signing — about a dozen slow-changing values that silently drive points. They are not in the monthly report and have nowhere to live in the current design.
  • The club's own view gains a breakdown. A club that can see 42 points but not which category produced them will ask an officer, every month. The trend graph needs a per-category split beneath it.

The report form itself stays as drawn, with one addition you asked for: a plain notes box beside the structured fields, for the presidents who will not fill a form. The officer receives structure either way, because the assist runs on their side.

4 · Decisions taken, and their consequences

Second factor is email OTPRemoves enrolment entirely, which is what made the authenticator route awkward for 146 accounts turning over each July. Costs a generous ten-minute expiry and a visibly rate-limited resend. Leaves src/lib/totp.js as dead code — delete it or keep TOTP as an officer-only option, but do not leave both half-wired.
Assist runs officer-side onlyA club never meets a model, so no suggested number can enter district records unreviewed. It runs where the workload actually is: one officer reading 48 months. Satisfies the presentation's tech-integration theme without an open-ended chatbot.
Editing team is a fourth rolePublish rights only: they change what the public sees and touch no club data, no reports, no accounts. Fits the spec's scoped-role model as an unscoped role with a narrow permission set.
Members submit showcase entriesPer the spec revision. Publishing still needs an officer, so the approval queue design holds — but its inbox now receives work from roughly three hundred people rather than 146, which raises the volume the edit-then-publish screen must absorb.
Anonymised top threeDrawn, not committed. Bars with no club names, own position shown only if in it. This is a district decision and the drawing exists so it can be made against something real.

5 · Open questions the design cannot settle

  1. Does the 6 September demo need any of this? The spec is a build plan for the year. The demo needs the public pages and a working login. Deciding what is demo scope versus year scope changes what I draw next.
  2. Who owns the point rules on day one? A configurable rule editor is a serious surface. If only the DRR ever touches it, it can be a plain table; if DSC officers will, it needs guardrails and the audit trail becomes visible in the UI, not just the database.
  3. Pasted links as the only asset path. With no bucket, a broken Drive link silently empties a showcase card or a charter certificate. Every image slot needs a designed fallback, and someone has to own re-linking. Worth confirming before it becomes forty broken images.
  4. Role transitions mid-year. The spec defers effective-dated roles, but a president changed mid-tenure this year. Until that exists, points and reports attribute to whoever holds the role now, not who held it then — an award dispute waiting to happen.
  5. Two lorem-ipsum constants are live. ExpandingCarousel renders BASE_PROJECTS — "Project Alpha", lorem body text — while the real five projects sit unused in FLAGSHIP_SLICES. One line to fix, and the highest-value change on the current homepage.
